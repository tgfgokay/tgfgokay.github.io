You've decided to grow your business, and now it's time for the formal side. Setting up a company in Şişli — or any district of Istanbul — is actually a more orderly process than you might think, as long as you take the steps in the right sequence. Below I've summarised, in plain language and with 2026 figures, the path I walk my clients through as their accountant.

## 1. First, choose the right company type

Not every type suits every business. Broadly, there are three options:

- **Sole proprietorship:** Fast and low-cost, with no capital requirement. A sensible entry point if you're starting small, on your own; as your earnings grow, you climb up the income-tax schedule.
- **Limited company:** The structure SMEs most often prefer. A balanced option for those who want partnership and limited liability. Profits are subject to **25% corporate tax**, regardless of size.
- **Joint-stock company:** If you're planning to take on investors, transfer shares or build a more corporate structure. Share transfers don't require a notary, and holding shares for two years brings a tax advantage on transfer gains.

This choice directly affects your taxation, your liability and your costs. Changing the type later can cost you both time and money — let's definitely talk before you decide. For the details, you can also look at the [sole proprietorship vs. limited comparison](/en/guide/sole-proprietorship-vs-limited/).

## 2. How much is the minimum capital in 2026?

For companies formed since 1 January 2024, the minimum capital amounts are as follows (Presidential Decree No. 7887, Official Gazette of 25 November 2023):

| Type | Minimum capital |
|---|---|
| Limited company | TRY 50,000 |
| Joint-stock company | TRY 250,000 |
| Non-public JSC under the registered-capital system | TRY 500,000 initial |

Two practical points:

- In a **limited company** there's no requirement to pay the capital in before registration; the subscribed amount is paid within **24 months** following registration.
- In a **joint-stock company**, **one quarter** of the capital subscribed in cash (TRY 62,500 on the minimum TRY 250,000) is blocked at a bank before registration; the rest is likewise paid within 24 months.

Don't inflate the capital just to "look strong": you're obliged to pay what you subscribe on time, and stamp-tax and fee items grow with the capital.

## 3. Prepare the required documents

Although it varies by company type, you'll generally need:

- Identity and residence details
- A business address (a lease or title deed; a virtual office is possible for some activities)
- Articles of association (for limited/joint-stock companies — prepared through MERSİS)
- Signature declarations
- For a joint-stock company, the bank's capital-blockage letter

I prepare most of the documents on your behalf; you only step in at the signing stage.

## 4. Registry and tax office procedures

For limited and joint-stock companies, formation is registered at the **Istanbul Trade Registry Office** following the MERSİS application; with registration the company acquires legal personality and its tax record is opened. A sole proprietorship has no trade-registry step; the tax office registration is done directly — if you'll operate in Şişli, your affiliated tax office will most likely be one of the offices around Şişli. After opening, the tax office carries out an on-site address verification. I track all of these steps and complete them without a hitch.

## 5. Post-formation setup

The job isn't done just because the company is open; the real order is built after this point:

- **E-signature / registered e-mail (KEP)** application
- Switching to **e-document** systems such as **e-invoice and e-archive**
- **Ledger certification** and setting up the accounting order
- Tracking the first **return and social-security** obligations (an employer social-security file if you'll hire staff)
- Depending on your activity, extras such as a municipal licence or chamber registration

## If you're young, a significant advantage: the young entrepreneur exemption

There's serious support for those under 29 who register as an income-tax payer for the first time (Income Tax Law, repeated art. 20):

- For **three taxation periods** starting from the year the activity begins, the portion of earnings **up to TRY 400,000 (for 2026)** is exempt from income tax. (The amount is tied to the second bracket of the income-tax schedule and is updated each year.)
- In addition, **Bağ-Kur premiums are covered by the Treasury for one year.**

This exemption applies only to a **sole proprietorship** (income-tax status); a young founder who sets up a limited or joint-stock company can't use it. In choosing the type, this alone can be decisive — let's run the numbers together.

## How long does it take, how much does it cost?

If the documents are ready, a **sole proprietorship is usually set up in 1–2 days** and a **limited/joint-stock company within a few business days.** The cost depends on the type, the capital and the line of activity — notary fees, trade-registry fees and announcement costs, ledger certification and the accountant's fee are the main items.
